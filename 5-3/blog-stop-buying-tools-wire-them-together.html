Stop Buying Tools. Start Wiring Them Together.

Andrew BryceFounder & CEO, Sixty Seconds·17 March 2026·4 min read
The Argument

Most small sales teams have enough tools. They just don't talk to each other. The data from your meetings doesn't flow into your CRM, follow-ups, or prep. That gap is where deals die.

A CRM. A notetaker. A prospecting tool. A sequencer. Maybe a conversation intelligence platform.

Five tools. Five logins. Five dashboards. And somehow you're still copy-pasting notes from your meeting transcript into HubSpot.

The problem isn't that small teams don't have enough tools. It's that the tools don't talk to each other.

Your notetaker records the call and gives you a transcript. Great. But it doesn't update the deal stage in your CRM. It doesn't draft the follow-up email using what the buyer said. It doesn't create the action items you promised. It doesn't brief your team on what happened.

So you do all of that manually. Which takes 30-40 minutes per meeting. Which means half the time you skip it because you're busy. Which means the CRM gets stale, the follow-up goes out late, and the deal quietly dies.

The Question to Ask Before Buying Any New Tool

"Does the data from my last meeting automatically flow into my CRM, my follow-up email, my task list, and my next meeting prep?"

If the answer is no, you don't need more tools. You need the ones you have to be connected.

This is the gap we obsess over at 60. Not replacing your CRM or your notetaker. Connecting them. The meeting recording flows into transcript analysis. The analysis flows into a follow-up email draft, CRM updates, action items, and a Slack brief. You review, approve, send. The system handles the plumbing.

Before you evaluate any new sales tool, read the AI Sales Tools guide we put together. It maps the 6 jobs your stack needs to do and recommends one tool per job. Because clarity beats choice when you're a small team.
