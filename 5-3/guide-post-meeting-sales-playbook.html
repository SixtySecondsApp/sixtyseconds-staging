Chapter 1

The 8-Step Overview

After a sales meeting, you should: write raw notes within 5 minutes, update your CRM within 15 minutes, send a personalised follow-up email within 4 hours, create action items, brief your team, schedule the next meeting, and set a follow-up cadence. Here is the complete system.

The meeting went well. The buyer was engaged. They asked good questions. Maybe they even said "this looks promising."

Now what?

For most reps, the answer is: get pulled into the next thing. The meeting notes go into a messy doc that nobody reads. The CRM update gets pushed to "later today" which becomes "tomorrow" which becomes "next week." The follow-up email - the most important email in the entire deal cycle - sits in a mental to-do list until the urgency fades.

This is the most expensive gap in sales. Not the pitch. Not the demo. The 30 minutes after the meeting that determine whether the deal moves forward or quietly dies.

This playbook closes that gap. Eight steps, in order, with time targets for each. Follow it after every meeting and you'll never lose a deal to post-meeting neglect again.

Chapter 2

Capture Raw Notes

Within 5 minutes

The first 5 minutes after a meeting are the most valuable and the most wasted. Your memory of the conversation is at its peak. Every minute you wait, details disappear.

Don't write polished notes. Write raw capture: the buyer's exact words on their biggest problem, the objections that came up, the names they mentioned, the timeline they referenced, the competitors they're evaluating.

What to capture:

  • Their #1 priority. What was the thing they kept coming back to? Not what you wanted to talk about - what they wanted to talk about.
  • Specific numbers. Budget range, team size, timeline ("We need this by Q3"), current spend on the problem.
  • Objections or concerns. What made them hesitate? Technical concerns, pricing, internal politics, competing priorities.
  • Decision process. Who else is involved? What's the approval path? Committee, boss, procurement step?
  • Competitors mentioned. Who else are they talking to? What do they like about the alternatives?
  • Exact quotes. The buyer's own words are gold for follow-ups. "We're drowning in CRM updates" is 10x more powerful in your follow-up than "you mentioned data management challenges."

The 5-Minute Rule

If you don't capture these details within 5 minutes, half of them will blur by tomorrow. Open your notes app, your CRM, or a voice memo. The format doesn't matter. Getting it down does.

If you record your meetings (and you should), the recording is your safety net - but don't rely on it as your primary capture method. You'll never go back and re-watch a 45-minute call. Capture the highlights while they're fresh, then use the recording to fill gaps later.

Chapter 3

Update Your CRM

Within 15 minutes

This is the step most reps skip. And it's the step that causes the most damage over time.

A CRM that's 3 days out of date is a CRM that lies to you. The deal stage is wrong. The next step is missing. The close date is a guess. Multiply that across 20 deals, and your pipeline forecast is fiction.

What to update:

  • Deal stage. Did the meeting move the deal forward? Update the stage. If the buyer agreed to a proposal review, that's a different stage than "discovery." Be precise.
  • Close date. Did the buyer mention a timeline? Update the projected close date to match what they actually said, not what you hope.
  • Next step. One sentence: what happens next and when. "Send proposal by Friday" or "Demo with technical team on March 20."
  • Deal amount. If budget was discussed, update the amount. If it wasn't, leave it. Don't guess.
  • Contact roles. Did new stakeholders appear in the meeting? Add them. Note their role in the decision.
  • Meeting notes. Paste your raw capture from Step 1 into the deal record. Future you will thank you.

The 15-Minute Rule

CRM updates should happen while the conversation is still in your head. Not at the end of the day. Not during your Friday pipeline review. Now.

If updating your CRM takes more than 5 minutes per deal, your CRM setup is too complicated. Simplify the required fields. The goal is fast, accurate logging - not a data entry exercise.

Chapter 4

The Follow-Up Email

Within 4 hours

Same-day follow-ups get 2–3x the response rate of next-day emails.

Not because speed is magic - because specificity fades. A follow-up written 2 hours after the meeting references what the buyer actually said. A follow-up written 2 days later defaults to generic filler.

This is the single highest-leverage action in the entire post-meeting process.

The formula: Specific reference from the conversation + one clear next action + easy reply.

Hi Sarah,

Good call earlier. When you mentioned that getting budget approval requires a business case showing 12-month ROI, that was really helpful context.

I've put together a one-pager with the ROI numbers based on what you shared about your current team size and deal volume. See attached.

Would Thursday at 10am work to walk through it together and discuss what to include in the internal business case?

Cheers,
[Your name]

This email works because "getting budget approval requires a business case showing 12-month ROI" is something Sarah actually said. It proves you were listening and shows you've already started working on her problem.

Speed vs. quality

Don't sacrifice specificity for speed. A specific email sent at 4pm beats a generic email sent at 2pm. But a specific email sent at 2pm beats both. If you can't achieve both manually, this is where AI meeting tools earn their keep - pulling the specific details from your recording and drafting the email before you've even thought about it.

Chapter 5

Create Action Items

Within 30 minutes

Meetings generate commitments. Commitments without tasks are just good intentions.

After every meeting, extract every action item, assign an owner, set a due date, and log it in a system you actually check. This sounds obvious. Almost nobody does it consistently.

What counts as an action item:

  • Anything you promised to send ("I'll send you the case study")
  • Anything the buyer promised to do ("I'll loop in our CTO")
  • Internal tasks triggered by the meeting ("Build a custom demo for their integration use case")
  • Calendar tasks ("Follow up if no response by Thursday")

Where to log them: In your CRM as tasks attached to the deal. Not in your personal to-do list. Not in a notebook. Not in your head. Tasks in the CRM are visible to your team, tied to the deal, and create accountability.

The Missed Action Item Problem

The most common way deals stall is not a "no." It's a forgotten promise. You said you'd send a case study. You forgot. The buyer notices. Trust erodes quietly. Capturing every action item within 30 minutes of the meeting ending is insurance against this.

Chapter 6

Brief Your Team

Within 1 hour

Your colleagues shouldn't have to ask "how did the meeting go?" The relevant people should receive a brief before they think to ask.

Who needs to know:

  • Your manager. A 3-line summary: what happened, what's next, any blockers. Managers hate being surprised. Give them the headline.
  • The technical team. If a demo, integration, or technical review was discussed, brief the people who'll deliver it. Include the specific requirements the buyer mentioned.
  • Other reps on the account. If multiple people in your org interact with this buyer, share context so nobody contradicts what was discussed.
  • Support or onboarding. For deals nearing close, loop in the team that will own the relationship post-sale. Early context prevents painful handoffs later.

How to brief: A short Slack message or email to the relevant channel. Not a meeting about the meeting. 5-7 sentences covering: what was discussed, what was agreed, what's needed from the team, and the timeline.

Meeting with Acme Corp (Sarah Chen, VP Sales)

Went well. They're evaluating 3 tools for post-meeting automation. Main concern is integration with their existing HubSpot setup. Asked for a custom demo showing the HubSpot sync.

Next step: Technical demo on March 20. @james can you prep a HubSpot integration walkthrough by March 18?

Budget: confirmed £5K-8K range. Decision by end of Q1.

That took 60 seconds to write and saves 3 people from having to chase you for updates.

Chapter 7

Schedule & Cadence

Schedule the Next Meeting

Same day

A deal without a next meeting is a deal without momentum. Before the day ends, send a calendar invite for whatever was agreed as the next step. Don't say "I'll send some times." Send the invite. Include:

  • A clear purpose ("Proposal walkthrough" or "Technical demo: HubSpot integration")
  • The right people invited (including anyone new who was mentioned in the meeting)
  • A brief note in the description referencing what was agreed

If a specific date wasn't agreed, propose one in your follow-up email. Give two options. Make it easy to say yes. Every day between the meeting and the next booking is a day where priorities shift, competitors advance, and urgency fades.

Set the Follow-Up Cadence

Same day

One follow-up email is not a process. It's a hope. After the first follow-up, plan the sequence.

The "Just Checking In" Ban

If your follow-up starts with "just checking in" or "bumping this up", you've failed. Every touchpoint must add value - a useful resource, a relevant insight, a question that advances the conversation. If you can't think of something to add, wait until you can.

Building a personalised cadence manually takes 10-15 minutes after every meeting. Chapter 8

Review & Checklist

Review and Prep for Next Time

The final step happens before the next meeting, not after the current one - but you set it up now. Create a "pre-meeting review" task for the day before the next scheduled meeting. When it fires, review:

  • Your notes from the last meeting (captured in Step 1, logged in Step 2)
  • The action items you committed to (tracked in Step 4)
  • Whether the buyer completed their actions (visible from your follow-up cadence in Step 7)
  • Any new information since the last meeting - company news, stakeholder changes, competitor moves

This review takes 5 minutes when your records are clean. It takes 30 minutes of panicked scrambling when they're not.

The Compound Effect

Each meeting builds on the last. Clean notes make the next prep faster. Tracked action items make the next meeting start with accountability. A maintained cadence means the buyer shows up already re-engaged. The first time you do this, it feels slow. By the fifth deal, it's muscle memory.

The Post-Meeting Checklist

Run through this after every meeting.

  • Raw notes captured (within 5 min)
  • CRM updated: stage, close date, next step, notes (within 15 min)
  • Follow-up email sent with specific reference + next action (within 4 hours)
  • Action items created with owners and due dates (within 30 min)
  • Team briefed via Slack or email (within 1 hour)
  • Next meeting scheduled or proposed (same day)
  • Follow-up cadence planned and logged (same day)
  • Pre-meeting review task set for next meeting
